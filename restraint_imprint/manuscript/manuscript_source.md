# The restraint imprint: how crystallographic refinement shapes deposited protein geometry

Wei Chen* and Xiaohong Lu

Louisiana State University Health Sciences Center, Shreveport, LA 71103, USA

*Correspondence: Wei Chen, wei.chen@lsuhs.edu. ORCID: Wei Chen, https://orcid.org/0009-0005-3547-0779. Xiaohong Lu, xiaohong.lu@lsuhs.edu

**Synopsis.** Restraint targets pass into refined protein coordinates through a coupled, predictable response, and the same imprint is written into the Protein Data Bank; geometry libraries learned or judged on deposited structures inherit it, and three rules for judging a library are applied to our own.

**Keywords.** crystallographic refinement; restraint libraries; protein backbone geometry; conformation-dependent library; validation; Protein Data Bank; benchmarking

## Abstract

Protein geometry libraries are learned from deposited crystal structures and judged against them, yet those structures were refined with earlier libraries. A deposited structure therefore records not only what the diffraction experiment observed but how the observations were interpreted. We measured that interpretation directly. In paired re-refinements of 19 structures, bond targets passed into the refined coordinates almost completely (86%) and an isolated angle target only half as much (48%). The difference was not resistance from the data: the three angles at the carbonyl carbon share a planar centre, and refinement removed any change to their sum while passing about four-fifths of the rest. Measured once in each structure, this coupled response forecast a library swap before it was made. Registered in advance, its prediction of the move from conformation-dependent (CDL) to learned MechLib targets erred by 0.064° on average, against 0.105° for assuming the targets are copied, and was better in 16 of 19 structures, most clearly at atomic resolution. The same coupling is written into the Protein Data Bank. PHENIX deposits acquired CDL's pattern at the software build that made it the default, and a 2018 CCP4 revision displaced an angle it never edited while leaving the angle sum intact. In consequence, each library agreed relatively better with deposits refined using it, and a library learned from deposits depended on the refinement programs in its training set. Judged by the same rules, our own library gained a little from side-chain rotamer information away from home but failed a registered quantum-chemical test on three bond lengths. Agreement with deposited geometry is not an independent test of a restraint library; forecasts and endpoints outside the refinement loop are.

## Introduction

A protein crystal structure is an interpretation. Diffraction data constrain atomic positions, while bond, angle, torsion and contact terms supply the chemistry that the data cannot determine on their own.[Engh1991,Evans2007] This division of labour is what made macromolecular refinement possible,[Murshudov1997,Murshudov2011,Afonine2012] and its cost has been understood almost as long: what refinement is allowed to change shapes what is deposited,[Kleywegt1995] and how far a model should depart from its targets is a judgement rather than a measurement.[Jaskolski2007]

The backbone sharpens that judgement because its covalent geometry is not fixed. Surveys of high-resolution structures showed that backbone angles shift with the torsions φ and ψ;[Karplus1996,Berkholz2009] calculations on isolated peptides reproduced the shifts without a crystal;[Jiang1997] and mapping their averages over the φ/ψ plane produced the conformation-dependent library (CDL).[Berkholz2009] As refinement targets, CDL improved geometry at little cost in R factors, even at atomic resolution,[Tronrud2010,Tronrud2011] and became the backbone default in PHENIX.[Moriarty2014,Moriarty2016] Fourier tables removed the bins,[Tronrud2021] learned internal-coordinate models added sequence context,[Int2Cart2023] and quantum-trained potentials replaced statistics with chemistry.[AQuaRef2025] Of these steps, only the peptide calculations neither learned from deposited structures nor were judged against them.

The same archive anchors the rest of structural biology. Validation statistics compare new models with earlier deposits;[Williams2018,Gore2017] force fields are tested in simulations started from deposited structures;[Lindorff2012] structure predictors are trained on the archive and reproduce its idealized geometry.[Jumper2021,Abramson2024,Tan2025] The loop is closed. A library contributes to refined coordinates, and those coordinates become training examples and benchmark labels for the next library. The usual safeguard does not open it: sequence-separated and future-deposition holdouts keep proteins apart, but they do not keep refinement conventions apart.[Roberts2017] Nor can the loop be read from history alone, because archive-wide quality has improved steadily over the same decades.[Brzezinski2020]

The builders of CDL anticipated part of this, selecting training structures with care,[Berkholz2009,Tronrud2021] and the debate over stereochemical targets has long weighed preserving chemistry against permitting data-supported deviations.[Jaskolski2007,Touw2010] Restraint influence on fitted parameters is also well described in theory, through leverage[Parsons2012] and the coupled derivatives of restrained least squares.[Bourhis2015] Our own analysis of backbone angle coupling found that structures refined with PHENIX and with REFMAC differed in a way that observational data could not attribute, and concluded that controlled re-refinement of identical diffraction data would be needed.[ChenLu2026] What has been missing is a measurement: how much of a specified change in a library appears in a refined protein, whether that response can be predicted, and whether its footprint can be found in the archive itself.

Here we make that measurement and follow its consequences. We change restraint targets while holding the data fixed, use the measured response to predict a real dictionary revision, find the corresponding footprints at documented library changes across the PDB, and ask what they mean for libraries learned and judged on deposited coordinates. Finally, we turn the resulting rules on a library of our own.

## Results

### Refinement passes restraint targets into coordinates through a coupled response


To isolate what the library contributes from what the data constrain, we re-refined 19 of 24 structures frozen in advance, spanning three resolution bands, after shifting CDL targets up and then down by the same amount, with model, data, free set and protocol unchanged (Figure 1A, Table S2). The coordinate change divided by the target change is the transmission.

Bond targets passed almost completely into the coordinates (86–87%), τ less fully (63%), and an isolated change to the Cα–C–N angle only about half as much (48%; Figure 1B). Resolution set its strength (Figure 1C). Every structure at 1.0 Å or better transmitted its bond, Cα–C–N and compensated edits less than every structure at lower resolution; isolated Cα–C–N passed 29%, 53% and 59% across the three bands. With fixed weights, part of this reflects a larger X-ray term rather than information alone.[Afonine2012,Karplus2012] CDL was known to change geometry at little cost even at atomic resolution;[Tronrud2011] such data also hold geometry closest to themselves.

The obvious reading of the angle result is that diffraction data resist angle changes more than bond changes. The geometry of the peptide unit offers another explanation. A bond can lengthen without disturbing its neighbours, but Cα–C–N, Cα–C–O and O–C–N meet at one nearly planar carbon,[MacArthur1996,Berkholz2012,Brereton2016,Peptide2025] so editing one of them contradicts the unchanged targets of the other two. If the data resist, a second edit should not help; if the neighbours resist, an edit that keeps their sum should pass more fully.

It did. When Cα–C–N was raised and O–C–N lowered by the same amount, Cα–C–N transmission rose from 48% to 77% in every structure (Figure 1D). Editing each of the three angles in turn gave the full response (Figure 1E). Whatever target was edited, refinement left the angle sum unchanged (to within 0.001° per degree of edit). Refinement removed the part of an edit that would change the sum and passed 82% of the remainder (residual 0.037; Figure 1F). Most of what looked like resistance from the data was the restraint network keeping its own books.

Library revisions change several targets at once, so we tested whether the matrix from isolated edits could predict a withheld combined edit. Prediction error was about one-third that of target copying across 19 structures, and lower in 18 (Figure 1G). However, this advantage had a boundary: at half the edit size in 4YCA, the matrix lost (0.039° versus 0.032°; Figure 1H). So the response is reproducible but not exactly linear. It is also a property of the refinement rather than of the library: it is set by the diffraction data and the restraint weights, not by the target values. A matrix measured once can therefore forecast a later change of targets, but not a change of weights.

### A measured response predicts a library change, until the library changes more than its means

The real test of a response is a change it was not measured on. In April 2018, the CCP4 Monomer Library revised its peptide-link geometry, raising the Cα–C–N target and lowering the O–C–N target at junctions preceding proline.[Vagin2004,Winn2011] We applied these paired changes to the before-Pro rows of the CDL table for 4YCA and re-refined. Response matrices measured previously from uniform edits predicted the coordinate changes at all 18 affected junctions without fitting to the outcome. The predicted response differed from observation by 0.004° on average and 0.07° per site, outperforming target copying, the restraint-only solution and a matrix without off-diagonal terms (Figure 2B–D). A matrix averaged over the structure predicted as well as the site-specific matrices, but one averaged over all 19 structures did no better than copying: the gain came from coupling measured in the same crystal, not from local detail. The comparison was post hoc, in one crystal; a forecast registered before refinement in all 19 structures is reported below (Figure 4F).

The archive revealed the limits of predicting a revision from target means alone. REFMAC deposits after the revision moved their before-Pro geometry in the documented directions, whereas PHENIX deposits, which do not use these links, showed no detectable change.[Murshudov2011] But early REFMAC deposits placed most of the change in Cα–C–O and O–C–N rather than Cα–C–N (+0.5°, +1.4°, −1.9°), a partition no prediction from the two edited means reproduced; later releases came closer (+1.2°, +0.5°, −1.7°; Figure 2E). The recovered dictionaries showed why a two-mean prediction is incomplete. The revision also stiffened the proline-link C–N–Cα restraint elevenfold, tightened other uncertainties, changed the ordinary link and added a Cα–C–O override to both links (Figure 2A). A change in stiffness moves geometry even where no target changes, in proportion to each restraint's existing deviation, and a matrix measured at fixed weights cannot see it. Whether these changes explain the early excess in Cα–C–O remains to be tested by implementing the full revision in refinement.

The archive nevertheless reproduced the central conservation result. The documented target contrasts changed the angle sum by −0.66°, yet the deposited contrasts preserved it (+0.003° in early releases, +0.001° in late ones), as in controlled refinement (Figure 2H). Cα–C–O carried the difference, moving by +1.4° although its target did not differ between the two links. This displacement was about half as large at atomic resolution (Figure 2F) and extended, more weakly and only in early releases, to ordinary junctions (Figure 2G), limiting the specificity of the proline effect. Allowing the pre-revision reference surface to vary within the bootstrap left the intervals unchanged (Table S3).

### The PDB records when PHENIX adopted CDL

PHENIX made CDL its backbone default at build 2155, a change tied to a software version rather than to any property of proteins.[Moriarty2016] We measured how closely each structure's angles followed CDL's φ/ψ pattern, as the within-class slope of deposited angles on CDL targets (0, not at all; 1, CDL's full amplitude). Before the switch, PHENIX deposits followed about half of CDL's amplitude; after it, nearly all. The change occurred at adoption (jump +0.27), was present at every bandwidth tested and disappeared when CDL's φ/ψ cells were scrambled within class, which keeps its values but breaks its map (Figure 3A–C).[Imbens2008,Lee2010] Relative to REFMAC deposits over the same years, the difference appeared only with adoption (Figure 3D).[Bertrand2004,Callaway2021,Roth2022] Every backbone angle changed, τ least (Figure 3E).

REFMAC deposits also changed, but differently. Their alignment drifted upward gradually, and in the 2018 dictionary era their τ pattern weakened while Cα–C–N and Cα–C–O strengthened (Figure 3F, Figure S1A).[Touw2010,Long2017] Different programs and library changes thus leave distinct marks in deposited geometry. Correcting an error in our junction-conformer assembly did not materially change the switch estimate (Figure S1B).

The switch provides a temporal test that is far less confounded than the program differences that limited our earlier comparison:[ChenLu2026] when PHENIX adopted CDL, its deposits acquired CDL's pattern.

### Learned libraries inherit the imprint, and benchmarks reward it

Surfaces learned from different refinement groups had similar shapes but different amplitudes (Figure 4A). Relative to REFMAC-trained surfaces, post-default PHENIX surfaces were 1.5–1.7-fold stronger for Cα–C–N and Cα–C–O and pre-default PHENIX surfaces about 0.7-fold; atomic-resolution deposits gave amplitudes close to the post-default set although CDL-refined entries were not enriched there. A learned library inherits the refinement history of its training structures.

Training-set composition therefore matters. Refitting MechLib on PHENIX-only or REFMAC-only structures moved the learned surfaces by about 0.2° on average, whereas balancing the two programs in the mixed set moved them by only 0.04° (Figure 4B). PHENIX-trained surfaces agreed better than the natural mix with held-out PHENIX deposits and worse with REFMAC deposits; REFMAC-trained surfaces were mixed (Figure 4C). A learned library reflects the refinement programs in its training set, and rebalancing a mixed set changes it only modestly.

The same imprint enters benchmarks. In comparisons specified before the data were read, CDL's advantage over the 2018 CCP4 targets was larger on deposits refined with CDL than on deposits refined with the CCP4 targets: the difference in differences was −0.24° for Cα–C–N and −0.69° for O–C–N, and for O–C–N the winner itself reversed (Figure 4D). Two CCP4 dictionary versions each agreed relatively better with REFMAC deposits from their own era for O–C–N (−0.21°), and CDL's lead over MechLib was likewise larger on PHENIX than on REFMAC deposits (−0.13° and −0.19°; Table S7). These comparisons are observational, because refinement populations also differ in era and protein mix, but each points the way the imprint predicts: part of a library's lead is being scored on structures it helped refine.

### Away from home, a library can still gain information

Real information should still show up on deposits a library did not help refine. Residues preceding proline occupy their own region of φ/ψ space,[MacArthur1991,Ho2005] and conditioning MechLib on the next residue nearly halved its before-Pro Cα–C–N error, although CDL, which has always made that distinction, remained ahead (Table S5). Side-chain rotamer was a second candidate. In a comparison fixed in advance on REFMAC deposits, which neither CDL nor MechLib refined, conditioning on χ1 lowered the error of N–Cα–Cβ by 0.036° and of C–Cα–Cβ by 0.023° (2–3%), but made τ slightly worse (Figure 4E). As the rule fixed beforehand required, the library we report uses χ1 for the two Cβ angles only. Against CDL on the same deposits it was better for N–Cα–Cβ and worse for C–Cα–Cβ and τ (Table S7): there was no overall winner.

### A registered forecast predicts a library swap

The transfer test of Figure 2 was made after its outcome was known. Before refining, we therefore registered predictions for replacing CDL's carbonyl-angle targets with MechLib's in all 19 structures, using each junction's response matrix from Figure 1 (5,940 junctions). The planned edits were small (median largest edit per site 0.48°), within the regime where the matrix had lost to copying in 4YCA, and the forecast was frozen before any swapped refinement was run.

The forecast met its registered rule. Its mean error was 0.064°, against 0.105° for copying the targets, and it was better in 16 of 19 structures (Figure 4F, Table S10). The angle sum was again conserved: the edits targeted a change of +0.016°, and refinement delivered −0.0001°. Most of the gain came at atomic resolution, where transmission is lowest; at lower resolution, copying was already nearly as accurate. Before adopting a library, a crystallographer can estimate how much of it a structure will take up.

### An independent quantum-chemical test gives a mixed verdict

Agreement with deposited geometry is not an independent test of physical accuracy. We therefore re-scored MechLib against 80 frozen constrained quantum-chemical geometries of capped residues, using a success rule registered in advance and unchanged; no new calculation was run.[Chai2008,Weigend2005,Holm1979] MechLib was closer than fixed AMBER values for every valence angle (τ 1.10° against 1.82°) and for two of five bonds (Figure 4G), and its aggregate weighted score was favourable, with 14 of 20 residues better than AMBER. But the registered test failed. Three bond lengths, N–Cα, C=O and the peptide C–N (mean offset −0.026 Å), were systematically further from the quantum-chemical values, as they were for MechLib without χ1 and for an earlier, pre-correction MechLib (Figure 4H, Table S6). Improved angles did not amount to success against the independent reference. Such offsets between crystal-derived and gas-phase bond lengths may affect any deposit-derived library; whether they affect CDL has not yet been tested.

## Discussion

### A structure is a record of its interpretation

The finding is simple to state and has been hard to see. Refinement writes its library into the coordinates through a coupled response that keeps the angle sum and passes most of the remainder, and the archive carries that writing at each documented library change we examined. Deposited geometry still contains molecular information; the point is that it contains it already interpreted. A benchmark against deposits scores a mixture of molecule, measurement and refinement practice, and the mixture differs between refinement populations.

Global statistics are poor guides to this mixture. Cross-validated R factors were designed to detect overfitting of the diffraction data, [Brunger1992,Brunger1993] and in our pilot refinements they did not distinguish arms whose geometry differed systematically, consistent with earlier CDL reports. [Tronrud2010] Map-based measures address the model–density relation rather than where a geometry target came from. [Read1986,Tickle2012] Re-refinement under consistent procedures, as in PDB-REDO, removes heterogeneity between structures, [Joosten2012,Joosten2014] but a uniform library imposes its pattern uniformly rather than removing it. The same caution applies to the validation distributions new structures are compared with [Chen2010,Williams2018,Read2011] and to predictors that learn idealized geometry from the archive. [Jumper2021,Tan2025]

### Prediction is more useful than correction

It is tempting to subtract a library's contribution from a deposited value. Coupling makes that unreliable: recovering what the data alone support means dividing by the fraction they retained, which amplifies noise most where transmission is highest, and every unedited restraint remains in the answer. Prediction asks less and delivers more. A response measured once in a structure forecast what a dictionary revision would do to it, without any claim that either outcome is the true molecule. That forecast is what a crystallographer needs before adopting a new library, and what a reader needs before comparing two structures refined with different dictionaries.

Its limits are now explicit. Registered in advance, it forecast a library swap in 19 structures, but its advantage over copying lay mainly at atomic resolution, and at half amplitude in 4YCA it lost. It addresses changes in target means, whereas real revisions also change uncertainties, and a change in weight acts in proportion to each restraint's existing deviation. The early archive response to the 2018 revision lies outside what a mean-only predictor can reach. A response measured at fixed weights after one macrocycle need not equal the stationary derivative of a fixed objective,[Parsons2012,Bourhis2015] and a synthetic multi-pass study found continued drift after several passes, mostly in waters, with backbone angles changing by about 0.01° per pass (Table S8). Paired refinements implementing complete revisions, weights included, will set the reach of the method.

### How to judge a new library

Three different questions are often answered with one benchmark: does a library reproduce previously refined geometry, does it agree better with experimental observations, and does it describe molecules better? Our results suggest three rules for keeping them apart, and we applied each to our own library. First, score away from home. Agreement with deposits is partly inherited, so it should be reported within refinement populations, and a gain should be claimed on deposits that neither library helped refine. There, χ1 added a small amount of predictive information for the two Cβ angles but none for τ, and no library won overall. Second, forecast before deploying. A response measured once in a structure predicts how much of a new library it will take up, and our forecast of the swap to MechLib was registered before it was tested. Third, use an independent referee, an endpoint that never passed through a restraint library. Ours improved every valence angle and still failed its registered rule on three bond lengths; the rule did its job by keeping that result from being read as success.

The same evidence bears on library construction. A learned geometry library depends on the refinement-program composition of its training set: single-program fits differed by approximately 0.2° on average, with a clear home advantage for PHENIX-trained geometry, whereas rebalancing the mixed training set changed the learned surfaces only modestly. The provenance of a training set should therefore be documented and stratified rather than assumed neutral, and coupled angle targets should be designed together, because refinement will reconcile them anyway. Models trained on electronic-structure calculations inherit their own approximations, as empirical models inherit refinement conventions;[AQuaRef2025,Dittrich2025] both should be tested rather than trusted for their origin.

### Limitations

The controlled response comes from one refinement program, one macrocycle and fixed weights, in 19 of 24 frozen structures; the matrix evaluation, resolution comparisons and the dictionary-revision transfer test were exploratory or post hoc, and the prospective swap changed targets, not weights. The archive analyses rely on header provenance, cannot recover each depositor's options or dictionary, and infer shipped dictionaries from version timing; a non-significant pretrend does not establish parallel trends, and the before-Gly negative control was not null. The home-advantage contrasts compare refinement populations that also differ in era and protein mix. Alternate-conformer exclusion changes the analysed population, and the excluded fraction is not an error rate. The context and amplitude analyses use entry-level separation and carry no intervals for surface slopes. The quantum-chemical referee covers capped residues in vacuum at four φ/ψ points, says nothing about dynamics or populations, and has not yet been applied to CDL.

Restraints are necessary, and the libraries examined here are among the most careful pieces of knowledge in structural biology. That is why their imprint matters. Knowledge carried by a reference structure has a history, and a new library can be judged fairly only by someone who reads it.

## Methods

Full protocols, counts, audit records and identifiers are given in the Supporting information (Detailed methods); the essentials needed to reproduce each analysis are given here.

### Structures and geometry

Structures came from PISCES-derived chain selections of the Protein Data Bank, with analysis-specific manifests fixed in advance.[Wang2003,Berman2000] Junction geometry required blank alternate-location identifiers for every contributing atom, which avoids combinations assembled from independently selected alternate conformers but also excludes valid alternate conformations. The corrected archive analysis retained 1,588,519 junction rows in 7,905 entries. Sequence components were built from an MMseqs2 screen at 30% identity and 80% coverage, with entries connected through any clustered chain kept together.[Steinegger2017]

### Refinement interventions and response matrices

Twenty-four structures, eight per resolution band (≤1.0, >1.0–1.5 and >1.5–2.0 Å), were frozen before any shifted refinement. Deposited observations and original free sets were used throughout, with French–Wilson conversion where only intensities were deposited;[French1978] no free set was regenerated. Refinement used PHENIX 2.2.1-6174, one macrocycle of individual-site refinement, fixed coordinate weight and no weight optimization, solvent update, peptide flipping or ω-CDL.[Adams2010,Afonine2012,Liebschner2019] Paired arms started from identical coordinates and observations and differed only in CDL targets edited in memory: Cα–C and N–Cα by ±0.010 Å, Cα–C–N and τ by ±1°, Cα–C–O and O–C–N in isolation by ±1°, and a compensated arm with Cα–C–N ±1° and O–C–N ∓1°. Lookup tracing confirmed the edited library path. Restraint identities, targets, σ values, reflections and flags were audited across arms; five structures that failed identical filtered support were excluded without replacement, and nine bond arms were recovered by a disclosed correction for targets rounded in output.

Transmission was [q(+h) − q(−h)]/2h for half-step h and measured quantity q. At each carbonyl site, the three isolated angle edits supplied the columns of a 3×3 response matrix; the compensated response, predicted as T(1, 0, −1)ᵀ, was withheld from the matrix. For the dictionary-revision test, the before-Pro rows of 4YCA's CDL table were edited by the documented 2018 contrast change (Cα–C–N +1.80°, O–C–N −2.45°) and each junction's matrix was applied to that edit without fitting; comparators were the structure-mean and 19-structure-mean matrices, a diagonal-only matrix, target copying and the planar restraint solution. The matrix evaluation and this transfer test were exploratory and post hoc. The 2018 dictionaries are commits a683db684eacb84ead595e3cd97c3f055f19b595 and 2bc9cc946452592783051b781b496a044d0155a8 of the CCP4 Monomer Library repository (https://github.com/MonomerLibrary/monomers).[Vagin2004,Winn2011,Long2017]

### Archive analyses

Alignment with CDL was the within-class slope b = Σ(q − q̄c)(t − t̄c)/Σ(t − t̄c)² of deposited angles q on nearest-node CDL targets t, averaged over τ, Cα–C–N, Cα–C–O and C–N–Cα. Local linear regressions used PHENIX build minus 2155 as the running variable, separate slopes on each side, a resolution covariate and bandwidths of 400, 700 and 1100 builds;[Imbens2008,Lee2010] class-preserving permutations of the CDL table tested specificity. An event study compared PHENIX and REFMAC deposits from 2011 to 2017 relative to 2014.[Bertrand2004,Callaway2021,Roth2022] For the 2018 revision, pre-revision reference geometry was fitted by residue class, next-residue category and 10° φ/ψ node, and before-Pro and before-Gly contrasts were compared between recorded REFMAC version groups.

### MechLib and held-out benchmarking

MechLib has one definition throughout. It is a table of 12 backbone terms (τ, Cα–C–N, C–N–Cα, Cα–C–O, O–C–N, N–Cα–Cβ and C–Cα–Cβ angles; N–Cα, Cα–C, C=O, C–N and Cα–Cβ bonds) on a periodic 10° φ/ψ grid per residue type, split by whether the next residue is proline and evaluated by periodic Catmull–Rom interpolation at bin centres. A cell uses its training mean when it has at least ten observations, otherwise the unsplit residue value, then a pooled value, then the global mean. For N–Cα–Cβ and C–Cα–Cβ only, residues with an assigned χ1 rotamer use a χ1-conditioned cell nested in the same grid, under the same rules. This is version 2 of the library (target array SHA-256 ef7b86b3…; χ1 model 6210897c…), trained on 5,926 chains; no other version is used. Held-out deposits (1,295 structures in 479 sequence components, deposited after the training cohort) were scored on the B30 subset, junctions whose local Cα B-factor mean was at most 30 Å² (Table S9).

χ1 was assigned to the nearest of g−, t and g+ (unassigned beyond 40°) after a same-atom gate. Its comparison, on REFMAC deposits, was fixed before scoring, together with the rule that a term uses χ1 only if its Holm-adjusted improvement passes. Home-advantage contrasts were differences in differences, [MAE(P) − MAE(Q)] on P's home deposits minus the same on Q's, for CDL against the 2018 CCP4 targets (PHENIX post-default and post-revision REFMAC deposits), the 2018 against the earlier CCP4 targets (REFMAC deposits of each era) and CDL against MechLib (held-out deposits). For training-mix sensitivity, MechLib was refitted with identical rules on PHENIX-only, REFMAC-only, natural PHENIX-plus-REFMAC and equally weighted training sets (96.4% of training structures carried a program label), and surface shift and held-out error were compared; no refit replaced MechLib.

### Prospective library-swap forecast

For every carbonyl junction of the 19 structures, the planned edit e = MechLib − CDL for Cα–C–N, Cα–C–O and O–C–N and its predicted response T·e from that junction's matrix were computed, and the prediction table's SHA-256 was recorded before any swapped refinement (Table S10). Each structure was then refined with these three CDL targets replaced by MechLib values (σ unchanged) under the Figure 1 protocol and audits. The primary endpoint was D, the per-structure error of target copying minus that of the matrix forecast; success required a 95% interval above zero.

### Quantum-chemical test

The registered comparison MechLib-QM-Q1-v1 comprises 80 constrained geometries of capped residues (ACE–X–NME; 20 residues at four φ/ψ points), optimized with ωB97X-D/def2-SVP in vacuum.[Chai2008,Weigend2005] Term errors were weighted by the square root of the force constant, averaged per case, and compared with fixed AMBER values. Success required a residue-clustered interval above zero, at least 12 of 20 favourable residues, no Holm-significant worse term and at least 68 quality-passing cases.[Efron1979,Holm1979] MechLib was re-scored against the frozen reference values without new calculations.

### Statistical conventions

Unless stated otherwise, intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap intervals from 10,000 resamples of the independent unit of each analysis. These units are structures within resolution bands for controlled refinements and the swap forecast; sequence components for archive and held-out analyses, with errors averaged within structures, then components, and components weighted equally; and residues for the quantum-chemical test. Paired comparisons used the same resamples and weights for both members of a pair, and Holm adjustment was applied within each prespecified family. Analyses are labelled as registered (specification hashed before data were read: rotamer conditioning, home advantage, training mix, swap forecast and the quantum-chemical test), prespecified within a frozen panel (the bond-minus-angle and compensated transmission contrasts) or exploratory (matrix evaluation, resolution comparisons, the 4YCA transfer test and context surfaces).

### Reproducibility

Input, code, model and output identities were recorded in manifests with SHA-256 checksums, and registered analyses were run from specifications whose checksums the code verified before reading data; amendments were made only before data were read. Source corrections and post-outcome changes were documented rather than backdated. Python analyses used NumPy, pandas, SciPy and Matplotlib.[Harris2020,McKinney2010,Virtanen2020,Hunter2007] A synthetic feasibility branch did not reach endpoint stability and is reported in the Supporting information.

### Figure 1 Refinement passes restraint targets into coordinates through a coupled response

Signed target interventions in 19 complete structures from a panel of 24 frozen before shifted outcomes were inspected (six at ≤1.0 Å, seven at >1.0–1.5 Å, six at >1.5–2.0 Å; blue shades). PHENIX, one macrocycle, fixed weights; blank-altloc standard-residue junctions; structures weighted equally. Diamonds and bars, means and 95% intervals from 10,000 structure resamples within bands. A, Design: the starting model, data, free set and protocol are identical in the paired arms; transmission is the signed coordinate contrast divided by the signed target contrast. B, Transmission for Cα–C and N–Cα (±0.010 Å) and isolated Cα–C–N and τ (±1°) edits: 85.7%, 87.4%, 47.5% and 62.6%; prespecified bond-minus-angle contrast +31.5 points (29.6–33.2). C, Band means (exploratory); fixed weights mean the band effect includes weight scaling. D, Cα–C–N transmission under isolated and angle-sum-preserving (Cα–C–N ±1°, O–C–N ∓1°) edits on identical junctions; lines join structures; 47.5% → 77.0%, paired gain +29.5 points (27.9–30.9). E, Mean three-angle response matrix (row, measured angle; column, edited target). F, Matrix entries against 0.82(I − J/3); columns sum to zero within 0.001; residual 0.037 (descriptive). G, Error predicting the withheld compensated response: matrix 0.053°, targets copied 0.170°; matrix better in 18 of 19 (exploratory, added after pilot inspection). H, Pilot 4YCA at ±1° and ±0.5°: the matrix lost to target copying at half amplitude. Five structures excluded for filtered-support differences (3Q46, 7Q0O, 6STL, 4KWD, 2GSV); nine bond arms recovered by a disclosed print-precision correction.

### Figure 2 A measured response predicts a library change, until the library changes more than its means

A, The April 2018 CCP4 peptide-link revision from hash-matched before/after snapshots: target changes for the proline (PTRANS) and ordinary (TRANS) links, with weight changes (1/σ²) where larger than 1.4-fold; a Cα–C–O override (120.6°, σ 1.5°) was added to both links. B–C, 4YCA re-refined with the PTRANS-direction edit (Cα–C–N +1.80°, O–C–N −2.45°) on its 18 before-Pro junctions: observed response per junction against prediction from each junction's isolated-edit matrix (B) and against target copying (C). D, Prediction error for seven predictors, per site and for the mean response vector; the comparison is post hoc (outcome known; nothing fitted to it). E, Change in the before-Pro minus ordinary-junction contrast in REFMAC deposits (version groups 5.8.0222–0267 and ≥5.8.0352 relative to ≤5.8.0189), with the documented target contrast and the controlled 4YCA response. F, The same contrast change by resolution band (exploratory). G, Before-Pro and before-Gly (negative control) contrasts; the ordinary link also changed, so the control is not untreated. H, Sum of the three angle changes: documented targets −0.655°, controlled refinement −0.003°, REFMAC deposits +0.003° and +0.001°. Archive intervals are 95% sequence-group weight-bootstrap sensitivity intervals with the pre-revision reference fixed; resampling the reference left them unchanged (Table S3).

### Figure 3 The PDB records when PHENIX adopted CDL

Corrected (blank-altloc) geometry throughout. A, PHENIX deposits by build (200-build bins; bootstrap 95% intervals). B, Local linear fits within ±700 builds of build 2155 (CDL default; release 1.10, 2 September 2015); points, 50-build bin means sized by count; jump +0.27 (0.19–0.37), sequence-group bootstrap, 871 structures in 852 groups. ### Figure 4 Three rules for judging a library, applied to our own

Figure 4 shows how deposits carry a library into training and testing (A–D) and applies three rules, including to our own library: score away from home (D, E), forecast before deploying (F) and use an independent referee (G, H). A, Amplitude of conformation surfaces learned from each refinement group or from the ≤1.0 Å tier, relative to REFMAC-trained surfaces (slope over shared nodes; correlations ≥0.95 for the backbone angles; corrected features, entry-level splits; no intervals). B, Mean absolute shift between learned MechLib surfaces over cells supported in both fits; whiskers to the 95th percentile. C, Training-mix sensitivity: held-out MAE of single-program and balanced refits of MechLib minus the natural PHENIX-plus-REFMAC fit, on PHENIX and REFMAC deposits (circles, Cα–C–N; squares, O–C–N). D, Home advantage: MAE of CDL and the 2018 CCP4 targets against PHENIX post-default deposits (CDL's home; 1,778 structures, 1,719 groups) and post-revision REFMAC deposits (the CCP4 targets' home; 558, 549), group-weighted, archive cohort. Difference in differences, [CDL − CCP4] at CDL's home minus the same at the CCP4 home: −0.24° for Cα–C–N (−0.831 − (−0.594)) and −0.69° for O–C–N, where the winner reverses; negative favours the home library. The other prespecified contrasts are in Table S7. E, χ1 conditioning, MAE with minus without χ1 on held-out REFMAC (primary) and PHENIX post-default deposits. F, Prospective swap from CDL to MechLib carbonyl-angle targets in the 19 structures of Figure 1 (5,940 junctions): per-structure error of target copying and of the matrix forecast registered before refinement, coloured by resolution band. G, Constrained DFT geometries of 80 capped residues: MechLib MAE divided by fixed-AMBER MAE by term; orange, Holm-significantly worse. H, Registered QM endpoint (weighted error, AMBER minus library; residue-clustered 95% interval) for an earlier pre-correction library, MechLib without χ1 and MechLib; each failed on the same three bonds. Intervals in C and E are 95% sequence-component bootstrap and in F structure bootstrap within bands (10,000 draws). Panels A–E are deposited-geometry endpoints and do not establish physical accuracy.
